101 Foundations

  • 1.1Risk is not the price movingFor Howard Marks, real risk is the chance of losing money for good, not how much the price goes up and down.2 min
  • 1.2Price, value and margin of safetyPrice is what you pay, value is what you get, and the margin of safety is the gap between them that protects you when you are wrong.2 min
  • 1.3First, surviveBefore trying to win big, rule number one is to stay in the game.2 min
  • 1.4The market pendulumMarkets swing back and forth between euphoria and panic, and they almost never sit still in the middle.2 min

201 Building a portfolio

  • 2.1Rebalancing with bandsRebalancing is not a calendar chore: it is a rule that only kicks in when something has really drifted.2 min
  • 2.2All at once or bit by bit?With a large sum, investing it all at once usually wins, but going in bit by bit on fixed dates protects you from yourself.2 min
  • 2.3Factors: what a stock is made ofA factor is a trait that many stocks share and that explains why they move together.2 min
  • 2.4The currency you measure inA return only means something if you know which currency you measure it in and how much prices went up in that currency.2 min

301 Analysis without fooling yourself

  • 3.1Valuing is telling a story with numbersA good valuation brings together a story about the company with numbers that make it possible, and even the most famous number can fool you.2 min
  • 3.2The huge market that fools youA market being gigantic does not mean the companies fighting over it are going to make money.2 min
  • 3.3Low risk is not the same as low uncertaintyNot being able to guess next year's earnings does not mean you could lose your money for good.2 min
  • 3.4Cheaper is not always saferIf the reason you bought stopped being true, the stock being cheaper does not protect you.2 min
  • 3.5Knowing a company is not having an edgeKnowing a lot about a company does not tell you whether its price already reflects everything you know.2 min

401 Big decisions

  • 4.1Can it be undone?A mistake you can fix costs you money. One you cannot take back can knock you out of the game.2 min
  • 4.2Rules, not willpowerYour biases do not go away because you know they exist. You hold them back with written rules and with someone who checks you.2 min

Schools of thought

There is no single way to invest.

There is no single way to invest. Six schools side by side: what they believe, where they fail and who argues with them.

Compare the schools →

  • 01You do not need to beat the market: it is enough not to lose to itBuy the whole market, pay as little as possible in fees and let time do the rest.3 min
  • 02The price you pay decides what you earnBuy pieces of good businesses only when they cost less than they are worth, and wait patiently for the market to notice.4 min
  • 03You do not need to guess, it is enough to know where you areThe market swings back and forth between euphoria and panic, and this school adjusts how much risk it takes depending on where it thinks the pendulum is.3 min
  • 04Do not predict: set yourself up to survive being wrongBecause extremely rare events happen more often than we think, this school puts almost all the money somewhere safe and a small part in bets where you can lose a little and win a lot.4 min
  • 05A few bets, well chosenIf almost all wealth is created by a few companies, this school tries to find them and bet big on them instead of spreading out across a hundred.3 min
  • 06Follow the price, not the storyWhat has been going up tends to keep going up for a while, so this school jumps on the trend and jumps off when it breaks.3 min
