For content creators, streamers and influencers

You crushed it this month. What about next month?

When you live off social media, money does not arrive like a paycheck. It comes in waves. The trick is not earning more in the good months, but making sure the bad months do not sink you.

How your work really works

Your boss is an algorithm. And it gives no notice.

What people see The month of the wave.

The brand deal, the video that blew up, the new car in your story.

What people don't see The slow months.

The taxes that have not been paid yet. The fact that all the income depends on a single platform.

No contract guarantees you anything. A platform changes its algorithm, a brand cuts its budget or your audience moves to another app, and your income drops by half without you doing anything differently. It is not bad luck. It is how this job works. And since usually nobody withholds taxes for you, the bill comes later, all at once, when you may have already spent that money.

How to manage money that goes up and down

Four rules worth more than a million followers.

1

Pay yourself a salary.

Open an account just for what you earn from your content: your "business". From there, every month you transfer a fixed salary to your personal account, always the same, whether the month was amazing or terrible. Good months fill up the business. Bad months drain it a little. You live calmly in the middle.

2

Set taxes aside before anything else.

If nobody withholds taxes for you, not every dollar that comes in is yours. Set aside the right percentage the same day you get paid. An accountant can tell you how much it is in your case. If you don't know, set aside too much and you will have some left over later.

3

A cushion of 6 to 12 months.

The 3-month rule is for steady paychecks. With income that goes up and down you need more: between 6 and 12 months of your fixed salary, in something safe you can withdraw quickly.

4

Don't depend on just one thing.

If all your income comes from one platform, it is like having all your money in a single stock. Diversify your income (another platform, your own products, work outside social media) and diversify your investments: do not put what you earn into the same industry that pays you.

Try it

How much can you pay yourself without running dry in a slump?

It starts at 100%: paying yourself the whole average. See what happens when the algorithm changes, and then lower it to 80%.

What builds up in the good months, beyond the cushion, is not for spending. It is for investing. That way a two-month wave turns into something that keeps paying you for years.
