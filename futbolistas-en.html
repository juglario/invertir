For footballers

Your career lasts ten years. Your life, sixty.

Earning a lot, fast, is a blessing. It is also the fastest way to end up with nothing, if you spend it as if it will last forever.

The number everyone shares and the real one

It is not that 8 out of 10 go broke. The worrying part is something else.

What everyone repeats 78%

of American football players broke or in money trouble within 2 years of retiring. It came out in a magazine in 2009 and gets repeated everywhere. It was never measured that way.

What a serious study measured 1 in 6

filed for bankruptcy within 12 years of retiring, in a study of the 2,016 players who entered the NFL between 1996 and 2003. At 2 years it was 1.9%.

And the finding that really matters: those who earned more and those who played more years went bankrupt almost as often as everyone else. Earning more was not the protection. The protection was how the money was handled.

Source: Carlson, Kim, Lusardi and Camerer, "Bankruptcy Rates among NFL Players with Short-Lived Income Spikes", American Economic Review, 2015. This is American football: there is no equally solid study of soccer players, but the mechanism is the same, a huge income that lasts only a few years.

Why it happens

Four things nobody explains when you sign.

1

You earn sixty years of money in ten.

At the top level, a career ends on average around age 34, after about 11 years of playing. Counting the lower divisions, it is much shorter. After that come 50 or 60 more years of life, with a home, food, kids and health. The question is not how much you earn this year, but how much that money lets you spend every year of your life.

2

Your lifestyle goes up fast and does not come down.

When you earn more, you spend more: house, cars, trips. The problem is that lowering that level later hurts so much that almost nobody does it in time. That is why fixed costs are the dangerous ones: a house you have to maintain, monthly payments, staff. A one-time splurge gets forgotten. A fixed cost follows you.

3

Everyone shows up: family, friends and deals.

When the money appears, so do the requests: helping family, which is fine, lending to a friend, putting money into someone's nightclub, restaurant or clothing brand. Helping is part of life, but without a clear limit it becomes a hole. And the deals people offer you because you have money are almost never the good ones.

4

Injuries give no warning.

A footballer works with their body. An injury can end or shorten a career overnight. That is why an emergency cushion and insurance are not for scared people. They are part of the job, like training.

The first 90 days after a big contract

Eight things. Check them off as you do them.

Your team

Three people who look after your money. And none of them manages it alone.

1

Accountant

Tells you how much to set aside for taxes and how to pay what is fair, legally.

2

Independent advisor

Charges a flat or hourly fee, not commissions. If they earn more when you buy a product, they are not independent.

3

Lawyer

Reads the contracts before you sign: with clubs, sponsors, partners and anyone who asks you for a power of attorney.

Golden rule: the person advising you cannot be the one making money from what they sell you.

The career ends. The money doesn't have to.
